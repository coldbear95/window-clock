How this prices

fair = Φ( (spot − strike) ÷ (σ · √t) )

The contract is a digital option: its price is the probability of settling above the strike. Three inputs decide that — how far spot sits from the strike, how long is left, and how fast BTC is moving.

σ comes from realized vol in the live tape, measured over 15-second returns rather than tick to tick — tick-level returns are inflated by bid-ask bounce, which would bias every fair value toward 50% and make the leading side look permanently overpriced. Effective t is 30 seconds short of the close because Kalshi settles on a 60-second average, so the outcome is largely fixed before the clock hits zero.

Implied vol is what Kalshi's own quote says σ must be. When it and realized vol disagree by more than double, the gap is probably the model's error and not the market's — that number turns red.

Trade only when the gap beats the fee. Held to settlement you pay one fee, not two — scalping out early doubles it.

Everything here is in the odds Kalshi shows you. On a $1 contract one point of odds is one cent, so an edge of +6 is six cents a contract. The log converts to dollars.
